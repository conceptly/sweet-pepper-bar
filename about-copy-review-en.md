## Main editorial call

The page has enough personality already. Keep the strongest phrases and give the paragraphs different jobs:

- Hero: introduce the place and invite exploration.
- Concept: explain what the guest can enjoy together.
- Story: show how the place became what it is, using the regulars' influence on the menu as evidence.
- Guests and Team: let the people and photographs carry the warmth.
- Careers and Location: be specific enough to be useful.

The current Hero, Concept and Story keep defending the kitchen/bar combination: “take each other seriously,” “a serious kitchen and a serious bar,” “neither waiting on the other,” “wasn't a hedge,” “isn't a compromise,” “highest form of hospitality.” One clear expression of the idea is enough. The more distinctive fact in the source brief is that guests persuaded the kitchen to keep seasonal favourites.

The old copy document's IA is historical: the build is **Hero → Concept → How it feels → Perks → Story → Guests → Team → Careers → Location → Entrance photo → Visit CTA**. There is no separate Mission section. The old photo-library proposal has become a link to VK albums. Follow the built structure; don't restore retired sections just to fit the old prose.

## Deliberately outside the rule

An alternative opening for the Story:

> Tabasco was our first chapter. With Sweet Pepper, we kept the heat and made more room at the table.

This deliberately breaks `design.md` §1.1 by letting the venue narrate an ordinary paragraph in first person. **I think it earns a test here:** this section actually is the founder's story, beside his photograph, so “we” has an identifiable speaker. A narrow, explicitly attributed founder-story exception could sound more natural than removing every first-person pronoun. It should not become permission for “our” headings everywhere. The main proposal above keeps the current rule; this alternative needs an author decision and should be labelled as drafted narration, not inserted as a verbatim founder quote.
